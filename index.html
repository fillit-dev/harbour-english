The idea

English is not a subject here. It is how the week happens.

Most courses sit you in a room and ask the town to wait outside. Harbour English does the opposite. You live in Wicklow Town — a harbour at one end, hills at the other — and the language arrives the same way the locals use it: over a counter, on a walk, in a story that ran longer than you expected.

Mornings are yours. Buy the bread. Walk the Murrough. Sit with a coffee until the names of things start to stick. Then Des meets you, and the afternoon becomes a careful, kind unpicking of everything you have just lived through.

“You do not come here to study Ireland. You come here to spend a week inside it, and leave speaking more like yourself.”

How the week is built

Three simple parts. Nothing institutional.

01

A home in town

You stay in a highly rated Airbnb — quiet, well-reviewed, close enough to walk the harbour, Main Street, and the shops. A real kitchen. A proper bed. The feeling of arriving somewhere, not checking into a school.

02

Mornings that belong to you

There is no roll call. Sleep, read, walk the pier, get lost on purpose. The town is the first half of the lesson. You collect the English you will need by needing it.

03

Afternoons with Des

A linguist who has taught English for twenty years, and a Wicklow man. Conversation, pronunciation, the sentence you actually need at work. A lesson might start at the table and finish on the harbour wall, still talking.

The stay

Highly rated rooms. Walking distance from the water.

We place you in Airbnb homes we would put our own visitors in — places with strong reviews, clean light, and a kitchen you will actually use. Not a campus. Not a host family who needs you at breakfast. Your own front door, in the town you came to learn.

Your teacher

Afternoons with Des.

Twenty years teaching English, at every level. These days the work is communication and pronunciation — mostly for professionals, and for third-level students who need to sound like they belong in the room.

He has a PhD in Linguistics, specialising in English spelling and pronunciation, and four years as a Siri voice engineer — training a machine to speak clearly, and Irish. If you build things for a living, that is the point: Des can talk your language. He is not the humanities teacher who smiles politely while you explain your job.

He used to walk people through Dublin and Rome as a tour guide, which is why an afternoon here still moves. And he has hosted on Airbnb himself, which is why the stay is treated as part of the week, not a booking on the side.

A typical day

The town in the morning. Des in the afternoon.

No two weeks are identical — weather, mood, and what you want from your English all have a say. This is the shape most days take.

  1. 08:30

    Coffee on Main Street

    Order it yourself. Stay long enough that the barista starts to recognise you.

  2. 10:30

    The Murrough, or the harbour wall

    Sea air and the vocabulary for weather you can actually feel. Wicklow does not do abstract.

  3. 13:00

    Lunch wherever the town takes you

    A café, a counter, a sandwich on the pier. You are off-script on purpose.

  4. 14:30

    Des arrives

    The morning gets unpacked. Then a longer stretch of talk — at the table, through town, or down by the boats.

  5. 17:00

    One last correction on the walk home

    Not a test. A thing to carry into the evening, and use.

  6. Evening

    The town, or a quiet night in

    A pint, a shop, a book. Tomorrow you do it again, a little freer.

Who it is for

Adults who already have some English, and want it to become living.

Right for

Professionals — including technical people who are tired of being taught by someone who cannot follow their work. Travellers, and anyone preparing for life in Ireland or Britain. Couples or friends who want to learn together. Guests who like a small town more than a language campus.

Not built for

Complete beginners who need the alphabet first. Children or school groups. Anyone looking for a thirty-desk classroom, a certificate on Friday, or a party hostel with a whiteboard.

The pace

Intimate on purpose: one guest, or two. Enough English to buy bread and tell a story is enough to start. Des meets you where you are and moves the week from there.

The place

Wicklow Town is close to Dublin, and nothing like it.

Under an hour south of the city, and you are in a working harbour town: Black Castle on the point, the Murrough stretching up the coast, Main Street still a street people use. Small enough that shopkeepers remember you by Wednesday. Big enough for a good coffee, a proper walk, and an evening that does not need a plan.

  • The harbour Fishing boats, the castle ruin, weather you can talk about honestly.
  • The Murrough A long coastal walk. The week’s best classroom on a clear day.
  • The hills Wicklow Mountains at your back. Glendalough for a Saturday, if you want it.

Questions

Before you write.

What English level do I need?

Enough to get through a simple conversation, even slowly. If you can introduce yourself and follow a patient speaker, you have enough. Des will not throw you into a debate on day one.

Is this a classroom course?

No whiteboard factory. Afternoons are taught — properly — but they happen in town as much as at the table. You will be corrected. You will also be walking.

Where do I stay?

In a highly rated Airbnb in Wicklow Town, chosen for reviews, quiet, and a walkable location. You have the place to yourself, or you share it if you come as a pair.

Can two people come together?

Yes. Couples and friends are welcome. Des can teach you together, or split the afternoon if your English sits at different levels. Say so when you enquire.

How do I get to Wicklow Town?

Fly into Dublin. From there it is a direct train or bus, usually under an hour and a half, or a short drive down the coast. We send exact directions once dates are set.

What if the weather is Irish?

Then you will learn the words for it properly. Lessons move indoors when they should. The town does not close because of rain, and neither do we.
